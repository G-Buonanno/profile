About

Measuring what we can't see, to understand what it does to us.

Giorgio Buonanno is Full Professor of Applied Thermodynamics in the Department of Civil and Mechanical Engineering at the University of Cassino and Southern Lazio (UNICAS), Italy. He earned his PhD in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Cassino in 1996, and from 2016 to 2019 held a tenured full professorship in the Department of Engineering at the University of Naples Parthenope before returning to Cassino.

"Airborne transmission is a recognized pathway of contagion — yet for decades it was rarely measured quantitatively at all."

His research sits at the meeting point of aerosol physics, environmental metrology and public health: how airborne particles — combustion soot, traffic emissions, exhaled respiratory droplets — form, disperse indoors and outdoors, and translate into exposure and disease risk. During the COVID-19 pandemic, his quanta-emission and infection-risk models, built with international collaborators, became widely used reference tools for assessing airborne transmission in shared indoor spaces.

Research Focus

Six threads running through three decades of work on air we share.

Aerosol Science

Indoor & Personal Air Quality

Particle number concentration, size distribution and personal exposure assessment in homes, schools and vehicles.

Public Health

Airborne Disease Transmission

Quanta emission-rate modelling and infection-risk assessment for SARS-CoV-2 and other respiratory pathogens.

Metrology

Environmental Instrumentation

Design, calibration and validation of diffusion-charger and optical particle counters for field monitoring.

Atmospheric Pollution

Combustion & PM Emissions

Emission factors and lung-dose/cancer-risk modelling for waste-to-energy plants, heating systems and traffic.

Radiological Aerosols

Radon & Natural Radioactivity

Radon-progeny/particle interactions and radon emission from soil, springs and groundwater sources.

Building Physics

Ventilation & Comfort

Ventilation strategies balancing energy efficiency, thermal comfort and healthy indoor air.
